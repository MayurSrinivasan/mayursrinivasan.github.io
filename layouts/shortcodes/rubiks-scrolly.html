Step 1 of 5 · The fixed core 1 configuration

1. The six centers never move

A 3×3×3 cube looks like 27 little cubes, but the very middle is just a plastic axle. Bolted to it are 6 center pieces, one sticker each.

Face turns only spin the centers in place: white is always opposite yellow, red opposite orange, blue opposite green. Turning the whole cube in your hand isn't a new scramble, so the centers contribute a factor of exactly $1$.

2. Eight corners, and a mod-3 lock

There are 8 corner cubies, each with 3 stickers. They can be shuffled into the 8 corner slots in $8! = 40{,}320$ ways.

Each corner can also sit in 3 twists, suggesting $3^8$ orientations. But every turn twists corners in a balanced way, so the total twist is always $0 \pmod 3$. Choose the first 7 twists freely and the 8th is forced: only $3^7 = 2{,}187$ are reachable.

3. Twelve edges, and a mod-2 lock

Between the corners sit 12 edge cubies with 2 stickers each, which can be arranged in $12! = 479{,}001{,}600$ ways.

Each edge can be flipped 2 ways, suggesting $2^{12}$ orientations. Yet turns always flip edges in even numbers, so the total flip is $0 \pmod 2$. The last edge is forced by the other 11, leaving $2^{11} = 2{,}048$.

4. The parity lock: divide by two

One quarter turn moves 4 corners in a cycle and 4 edges in a cycle. A 4-cycle is three swaps, which makes it an odd permutation.

So every turn flips corner parity and edge parity together, and they must always match. You can never swap just two corners on their own; two edges must swap too. Exactly half the arrangements vanish.

5. Forty-three quintillion

Multiply it all out and you get 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 reachable states.

Make one 57 mm cube for every state, line them up, and the row stretches about 260 light-years, more than halfway to Polaris.
